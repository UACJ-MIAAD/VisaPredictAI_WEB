Exploratory data analysis (EDA)

An automatic, series-by-series census of the full panel: 27,911 observations across 194 structural series (136 with at least one specific date and 74 evaluable), over 298/298 bulletins since December 2001. 58 % of the panel is trainable (16,117 trainable obs, status F); the census also documents 382 historical retrogressions and the 1,659 Diversity Visa (DV) lottery rows. The ADF, KPSS and DF-GLS stationarity tests indicate that 71 of the 74 evaluable series require differencing. An annotated gallery of eleven figures accompanies the census —from the full panel’s regime map to the family trajectories, the FAD–DFF gap and the fiscal-year pulse. The full report is a downloadable PDF, regenerated automatically with every new bulletin.

The gallery’s annotated findings: in about half of the months with a date the line does not move —the reason the naïve forecast is so hard to beat—; retrogressions are administrative earthquakes that can erase more than a decade in a single bulletin (the worst episode: Mexico · F3, August 2006); today’s deepest backlogs exceed a quarter century in the family block (Mexico · F4) and a decade in the employment block (India); the filing calendar (DFF) always precedes final action (FAD) by months; the fiscal year barely has a pulse —no exploitable seasonality: a finding, not a shortcoming—; no area leads another (the best cross-correlation between countries occurs at lag zero, with no leading indicators); and no evaluable series is stationary in level: the panel is integrated of order 1.
